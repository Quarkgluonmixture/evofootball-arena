### in plain football language

The user played world 12 and wrote three sentences: **bodies crowd each other**; **passes cannot
get out**; **the ball hits a teammate's body and bounces off, or goes to an opponent.**

Everything this arc has built since then aims at the third one. PT-C0 measured it: the receiver
meets the ball on his **side or back** about two times in three. Three shut doors were then put
in the tree, one at a time, and none of them has ever been opened on a pitch together:

* **the pre-cue (3a)** — a body the passer is squarely facing reacts to the strike faster,
  because he half expected it;
* **the facing price (BF)** — turning your hips away from where you are running now costs you
  speed, so turning is finally a decision and not free glue;
* **the READY limb (3b)** — a receiver who believes a teammate is winding up onto him **turns
  his body toward him before the ball is struck**, but only if the turn is worth what it costs.

This exam opens all three together, on world 12's own composition, and asks the three questions
the user's own sentences ask.

1. **Does the ball stop hitting his side and his back?** That is (a) — the user's third
   sentence, measured as PT-C0 measured it.
2. **Does it hit MORE opponents?** That is (b) — his second sentence must not get worse.
3. **Is it still football?** Goals inside a band, passing not worse, turnovers not up.

Beside those, gating nothing: **how often a body actually turned** (the READY coverage — how
often he believed, how often he won the trade, how often the turn was really taken), **what 3a
